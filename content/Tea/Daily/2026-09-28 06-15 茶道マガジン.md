# 結び目から器へ——茶入を包む仕覆の時間

秋分を過ぎたころ。今朝は茶入そのものではなく、その外側に添う仕覆を見つめます。裂を裁ち、器の形に沿わせ、紐で口を結ぶ小さな袋は、保管のための脇役にとどまりません。結び目がほどかれるとき、器はただ現れるのではなく、席の時間の中へ迎え入れられます。

## 今日の見どころ

- 仕覆は茶入などに添う袋で、本体を守ると同時に、裂・仕立て・紐の取り合わせも鑑賞の対象になります。
- 箱、袋、蓋、銘など、本体に付属して伝わる一式は「次第」と呼ばれ、道具の来歴を担います。
- 京都国立博物館は、所有者が変わるたびに仕覆が誂えられた例を紹介しており、包みもまた道具の生涯に加わることが分かります。

## 日本語特集

濃茶の席で茶入を見るとき、視線は陶土の色、肩の張り、胴を流れる釉景へ向かいがちです。けれども茶入は、裸のまま席へ運ばれてくるとは限りません。裂で仕立てた仕覆に収まり、箱や蓋、銘にまつわる記録などとともに受け継がれてきました。京都国立博物館は、このように本体へ付属する一式を「次第」と説明しています。道具は一個の物体としてだけでなく、それを守り、名づけ、伝えてきた物々との関係の中にあるのです。

仕覆には、まず保護という明快な働きがあります。小さな陶製の茶入を包み、ほかの物との接触や擦れをやわらげる。しかし、働きはそれだけではありません。布の文様と色、織りの光沢、底の取り合わせ、口を絞る紐までが、茶入の形や釉調と響き合います。硬い焼物を柔らかな裂が囲み、冷たく見える釉に糸の温度が添う。異なる素材が重なることで、茶入の見え方も変わります。

古い裂のうち、とくに茶の湯の世界で尊ばれてきたものは「名物裂」と呼ばれます。京都国立博物館は、僧侶がもたらした袈裟や交易で舶載された染織品が、やがて著名な茶器や掛物を飾る名物裂として愛玩された歴史を紹介しています。一片の裂には、織られた土地だけでなく、海を渡る交易、日本での裁断と仕立て、茶人による見立てが重なります。仕覆は小さいながら、広い地理と長い時間を畳み込んだ工芸です。

興味深いのは、仕覆が必ずしも制作当初から変わらない一枚ではないことです。京都国立博物館の「茶の湯の道具と次第」は、制作時から添う共箱だけでなく、所有者が変わるたびに誂えられた仕覆にも触れています。新しい持ち主は、古い器を自分の好みだけで塗り替えるのではありません。器の格、形、伝来を読み、その器にどの裂を添えるかを選びます。その選択が、前の時代を消さずに新しい層を加えます。

茶入の鑑賞も、本体だけでは完結しません。同館の茶入展示案内は、産地、形、釉流れを基本的な見どころとしながら、付属する仕覆、牙蓋、盆、銘も魅力の一部に挙げています。これは付属品を豪華さの証明として数える、ということではないでしょう。器がどのように扱われ、何と組み合わされ、どの名で記憶されてきたかを読むための手がかりです。

席中で仕覆を扱うとき、紐をほどく動きには順序があります。ここで大切なのは、独習で流儀ごとの手順を断定することではなく、包みが開くまでに時間が生まれる点です。結び目へ手をかけ、紐が緩み、裂の口が開き、器が姿を現す。数秒の移行があるからこそ、客の注意は「まだ見えないもの」から「いま現れたもの」へ静かに集まります。仕覆は視線の速度を整える装置でもあります。

また、包むことは隠すことと同じではありません。何でもすぐ見えるように並べると、情報は増えても、出会いの輪郭は薄くなることがあります。仕覆は一度器を見えなくし、触れ方と現れる順序を定めます。隠すことで価値を誇張するのではなく、見る前に待つ時間をつくる。その慎みが、器をよく見るための余白になります。

今日は、身近な一つの器と一枚の布を並べてみましょう。貴重な茶入や古裂を自己流で包む必要はありません。日常の小さな器で、硬さと柔らかさ、色と文様、包む前と開いた後の印象を比べます。工芸を見るとは、完成品を一つずつ孤立させて眺めることではなく、物と物のあいだに生まれる関係を読むことでもあるのです。

## 小さな実践

1. 傷つける心配のない小さな器と、清潔な布を一枚選びます。
2. 器だけを見て、形、色、表面の印象を三語で記します。
3. 布を隣に置き、包まずに、色や文様が器とどう響くかを観察します。
4. 安全を確かめて軽く包み、ゆっくり開きます。開く前後で視線の速さがどう変わったかを一文にします。
5. 古裂、漆器、貴重な茶道具では試さず、正式な仕覆の扱いは先生や流儀の指導に従います。

## Full English Edition

# From the Knot to the Vessel — The Time Held by a Tea-Caddy Pouch

Just after the autumn equinox, this morning we look not at the tea caddy alone but at the *shifuku* that accompanies it. Cut from textile, fitted to the vessel, and closed with a cord, this small pouch is more than a supporting object used for storage. When its knot is loosened, the vessel does not merely become visible; it is welcomed into the time of the gathering.

## Today’s Highlights

- A *shifuku* is a fitted pouch accompanying a tea caddy or another treasured utensil. It protects the object, while its textile, tailoring, and cord are also appreciated.
- The box, pouch, lid, name, and other items transmitted with a utensil may form its *shidai*: the accompanying ensemble that carries the object’s history.
- The Kyoto National Museum notes that pouches could be newly commissioned as ownership changed, showing that the wrapping itself could join the continuing life of a utensil.

## Feature

When viewing a ceramic tea caddy used for thick tea, attention naturally moves toward the color of its clay, the tension of its shoulders, and the glaze flowing down its body. Yet a tea caddy has not always entered the gathering bare. It may rest inside a textile pouch and be handed down with a box, a lid, a name, and related records. The Kyoto National Museum calls such an ensemble of accompanying articles a *shidai*. A utensil exists not only as a single material object but also within relationships formed by the things that have protected, named, and transmitted it.

Protection is the pouch’s clearest function. It encloses a small ceramic vessel and softens contact and abrasion. Its work, however, does not end there. The textile’s pattern and color, the sheen of its weave, the construction of its base, and the cord closing its mouth may all answer the shape and glaze of the tea caddy. Soft cloth surrounds hard ceramic; the warmth of thread is set beside the cool appearance of glaze. When these materials meet, the vessel itself appears differently.

Historic textiles especially prized in tea culture are known as *meibutsu-gire*. The Kyoto National Museum describes how textiles carried by monks or imported through trade were eventually treasured as celebrated fabrics used with famous tea utensils and hanging scrolls. A small fragment can therefore hold several journeys at once: the place where it was woven, maritime exchange, cutting and tailoring in Japan, and a tea practitioner’s act of selection. Though physically small, the pouch folds broad geography and long time into a work of craft.

Significantly, a pouch was not necessarily one unchanging object present from the beginning. In its exhibition on utensils and their accompaniments, the museum distinguishes an original fitted box from pouches commissioned as ownership changed. A new owner did not need to erase an old vessel by imposing an entirely new identity. Instead, the owner could study its standing, form, and transmission, then choose a textile to accompany it. That choice added a new layer without removing the earlier ones.

The appreciation of a tea caddy likewise extends beyond its ceramic body. In an introduction to an exhibition of tea caddies, the museum lists place of production, form, and glaze flow as basic points of attention, then identifies the pouch, ivory lid, tray, and poetic name among the object’s further attractions. This need not mean counting accessories as evidence of luxury. Each accompaniment helps us read how the vessel was handled, combined with other objects, and remembered.

The act of opening a pouch creates a sequence. The precise formal movements differ by tradition and should be learned from a qualified teacher, but we can still notice the time produced by the covering. A hand approaches the knot; the cord loosens; the mouth of the textile opens; the vessel appears. Because this transition lasts for several seconds, attention gathers quietly from “not yet visible” toward “now present.” The pouch regulates the speed of looking.

To wrap is therefore not simply to hide. When everything is immediately visible, information may increase while the outline of an encounter grows faint. A pouch first removes the vessel from sight and establishes how and when it will appear. Its purpose is not to inflate value through secrecy but to make a moment of waiting before sight. That restraint creates room for closer attention.

Today, place one familiar vessel beside one piece of cloth. There is no need to handle a valuable tea caddy or historic textile without guidance. With an ordinary, safe object, compare hardness and softness, color and pattern, and the impressions before covering and after opening. To look at craft is not only to examine finished objects in isolation. It is also to read the relationships that arise between them.

## A Small Practice

1. Choose one small, sturdy vessel and one clean piece of cloth.
2. Look at the vessel alone and describe its form, color, and surface in three words.
3. Place the cloth beside it without wrapping, and observe how their colors and patterns interact.
4. If safe, cover the vessel lightly and open it slowly. Write one sentence about how the speed of your attention changed.
5. Do not practice on historic textiles, lacquerware, or valuable tea utensils. Learn formal handling from your teacher and tradition.

## 今日のことば — Vocabulary (12)

1. **仕覆（しふく） — fitted utensil pouch:** 茶入などに添え、保護と装いを担う裂製の袋。A textile pouch fitted to a tea caddy or another valued utensil.
2. **茶入（ちゃいれ） — ceramic tea caddy:** 主に濃茶用の抹茶を入れる陶製の器。A ceramic container principally used for matcha prepared as thick tea.
3. **裂（きれ） — textile fragment / cloth:** 仕覆などに用いられる織物の一片。A piece of woven material used for objects such as pouches.
4. **名物裂（めいぶつぎれ） — celebrated historic textile:** 茶の湯で尊ばれてきた由緒ある染織品。A prestigious historic textile treasured in tea culture.
5. **次第（しだい） — accompanying ensemble:** 箱や仕覆など、道具本体に付属して伝わる一式。The ensemble of boxes, pouches, and related objects transmitted with a utensil.
6. **共箱（ともばこ） — original fitted box:** 制作者や制作当初に関わって道具へ添えられた箱。A fitted box associated with the maker or the object’s original production.
7. **誂える（あつらえる） — commission / have made:** 目的や寸法に合わせて特別に作らせること。To have something specially made for a purpose or size.
8. **牙蓋（げぶた） — ivory lid:** 茶入の口に合わせて作られた象牙製の蓋。An ivory lid fitted to the mouth of a tea caddy.
9. **釉景（ゆうけい） — glaze scenery:** 釉薬の流れや色むらがつくる見どころ。Visual effects formed by the flow and variation of glaze.
10. **伝来（でんらい） — transmission / provenance:** 道具が人から人へ受け継がれてきた来歴。The history of an object’s passage from one custodian to another.
11. **取り合わせ（とりあわせ） — considered combination:** 複数の道具や素材を選び、調和させること。The considered selection and combination of objects or materials.
12. **結び目（むすびめ） — knot:** 紐などを結んでできる部分。The part formed by tying a cord or string.
