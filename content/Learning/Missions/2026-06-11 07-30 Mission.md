# Daily Learning Mission — 2026-06-11

[[Home]]

## Mission Title
Observe a Live ISS Pass and Explain the Orbit

## Objective
Spend 30–60 minutes learning how low Earth orbit works by predicting one International Space Station pass over Japan, observing it if timing works, and writing a short explanation of why the pass was visible.

## Exact Steps
1. Open Heavens-Above or NASA Spot the Station and set your location in Japan.
2. Find the next visible ISS pass and note the time, duration, max elevation, and direction of travel.
3. Spend 10 minutes reading a short source on low Earth orbit and why the ISS is visible around dawn/dusk.
4. If the pass happens today, step outside and observe for a few minutes; if not, use the next listed pass as your case study.
5. Write a 5–8 sentence note explaining:
   - why the ISS does not fall straight down,
   - why it appears only at certain times,
   - how elevation affects visibility.
6. Add one simple sketch or bullet timeline of the pass (start direction → highest point → end direction).

## Expected Output Artifact
A short Obsidian note section with the ISS pass details, your 5–8 sentence explanation, and a small sketch/timeline.

## Stretch Challenge
Compare the ISS pass with a Starlink visibility prediction and write 3 bullets on how their appearance differs for an observer on the ground.
