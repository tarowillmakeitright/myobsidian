## 1. Fed raises rates for the first time since 2023

**Why it matters:** The quarter-point increase takes the benchmark rate to about 3.9%, signaling that persistent inflation still outweighs political pressure for cuts. Expect knock-on effects for bonds, the dollar, equities, mortgages, auto loans, and credit cards.

**Source:** [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/f0ea070bd8d0d6bfa88203b62c33fed7)

## 2. U.S. House again votes to end the Iran war

**Why it matters:** The 220–204 war-powers vote shows growing bipartisan pressure over a seven-month conflict that AP says has cost more than $38 billion and is pushing up energy prices and inflation. The resolution still faces major procedural and veto hurdles.

**Source:** [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/8519dae8197214b4edf2192e174c82f4)

## 3. House passes sweeping Russia sanctions package

**Why it matters:** The 262–159 vote sends the most ambitious U.S. Ukraine-support measure in nearly two years to President Trump. It targets Russian officials and major parts of the economy; businesses and investors should watch implementation and secondary-tariff exposure.

**Source:** [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/f377fc956c442195dfcf50571b01a1c4)

## 4. Google opens smart-home control to third-party AI agents through MCP

**Why it matters:** Home MCP can let compatible agents inspect home activity, control devices, and build dashboards. This is a practical expansion of agent interoperability—but connecting household data and controls makes permissions, auditability, and household consent immediately important.

**Source:** [Google Home Developers](https://developers.home.google.com/mcp/home)

The rollout begins with Pro and Max, making it worth testing for report and slide workflows while retaining export and version-control safeguards.
