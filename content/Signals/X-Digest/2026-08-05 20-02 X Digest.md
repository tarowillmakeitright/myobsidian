## 1. EU AI transparency rules are now enforceable

**Why it matters:** Since 2 August, providers and deployers serving the EU must disclose direct AI interaction and, in covered cases, mark AI-generated or manipulated content and label deepfakes. This is an immediate product, content-pipeline, and compliance checkpoint—not a future policy proposal. Teams shipping into Europe should audit user notices, provenance metadata, and deepfake/public-interest content labels now.

**Source:** [European Commission — Article 50 transparency guidelines](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-publishes-guidelines-transparency-obligations-providers-and-deployers-certain-ai-systems)

## 2. A spent Falcon 9 upper stage was predicted to strike the Moon today

**Why it matters:** Researchers expected the impact near Einstein Crater at about 06:35 UTC on 5 August and coordinated professional and amateur observations. Beyond the spectacle, the event is a live test case for locating lunar impacts, studying ejecta and dust, and understanding hazards from artificial space debris. Watch for confirmed observations and datasets rather than unverified impact clips.

**Source:** [Fernando et al. — observational planning paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14625)

## 3. Europe’s €20B AI gigafactory push is moving toward procurement

**Why it matters:** The EU says 77 proposals across 16 member states responded to its early expression of interest, with a formal tender expected in summer 2026 and first construction targeted for 2027. This creates an actionable watchlist for compute infrastructure, energy, cooling, networking, construction, and financing vendors—and a strategic signal that sovereign AI capacity is becoming industrial policy.

**Source:** [European Commission — AI Gigafactories](https://commission.europa.eu/topics/competitiveness/competitiveness-coordination-tool-projects/ai-gigafactories_en)
