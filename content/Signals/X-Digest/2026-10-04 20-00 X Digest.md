## 1. Brazil votes in a high-stakes presidential election

**Why it matters:** Latin America's largest economy is choosing between incumbent Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and Flávio Bolsonaro. A candidate needs more than 50% to avoid an October 25 runoff; the outcome could materially shift Brazil's domestic policy and its relationships with the U.S. and China.

**Source:** [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/c194f8965632c8af9bcc4e7a3716cd7c)

## 2. Weak U.S. jobs growth changes the rate outlook

**Why it matters:** U.S. employers added a net 29,000 jobs in September, below expectations and sharply slower than August. Stocks rose as the report reduced immediate inflation fears, but the combination of weak hiring, elevated inflation, and high bond yields means rate-sensitive investors and businesses should watch upcoming Fed guidance closely.

**Source:** [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/a2b99562febc21d84955e243c87f5d31)

## 4. U.S. executive branch formally adopts “Super Intelligence” terminology

**Why it matters:** A new executive order directs federal departments and agencies to use “Super Intelligence” or “SI” instead of “Artificial Intelligence” or “AI” in official communications and policy documents. Vendors, policy teams, and researchers working with U.S. agencies should expect terminology and procurement documentation to change.
