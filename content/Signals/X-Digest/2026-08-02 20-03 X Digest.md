## 1. EU AI Act transparency rules take effect today

**Why it matters:** Article 50 now requires covered AI providers and deployers to disclose when people interact with AI and to make certain AI-generated or manipulated content detectable or visibly labelled. Teams serving EU users should audit chatbot disclosures, synthetic-media marking, deepfake labels, and public-interest AI text now; legacy systems placed on the market before today receive only a limited transition for machine-readable marking until 2 December 2026.

**Source:** [European Commission — Article 50 transparency guidelines](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/guidelines-transparency-obligations-providers-and-deployers-ai-systems)

## 2. EU enforcement of general-purpose AI obligations begins

**Why it matters:** The European Commission can now enforce the AI Act obligations for general-purpose AI providers, including technical documentation, downstream information, copyright-compliance policies, and public summaries of training content. AI vendors and businesses procuring foundation models should verify compliance evidence and contractual responsibilities rather than relying on roadmap promises.

**Source:** [European Commission — GPAI provider obligations](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/guidelines-obligations-general-purpose-ai-providers)

## 3. Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak remains an international health emergency

**Why it matters:** WHO continues to list the DRC–Uganda outbreak as a Public Health Emergency of International Concern. This is actionable for health organizations and travelers with exposure to affected areas: follow official health guidance, strengthen case detection and infection control, and avoid amplifying unverified outbreak claims circulating on social media.

**Source:** [WHO — outbreak assessment and recommendations](https://www.who.int/emergencies/disease-outbreak-news/item/2026-DON602)
