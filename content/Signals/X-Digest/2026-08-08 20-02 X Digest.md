## 1. Strait of Hormuz negotiations are the dominant global risk signal

**Why it matters:** Roughly 20% of the world’s oil normally moves through the strait. Iran is treating control of the route as strategic leverage, so any genuine breakthrough—or breakdown—in talks can quickly move oil, inflation expectations, shipping costs, and Asian import markets. **Action:** Watch confirmed U.S./Iran statements and Brent crude rather than viral claims about an agreement.

**Source:** [AP — Iran’s leaders think they have Trump cornered, but their strategy carries great risks](https://apnews.com/article/77d91b276db6d749711619b0aa1c5c3f)

## 2. U.S. payrolls unexpectedly contracted, complicating the Fed outlook

**Why it matters:** Employers cut 23,000 jobs in July, sending stocks higher and Treasury yields lower as traders reduced near-term rate-hike expectations. The actionable tension is that weaker employment argues for easier policy while energy-driven inflation argues for restraint. **Action:** Reassess rate-sensitive positions and watch the next CPI and jobless-claims releases.

**Source:** [AP — U.S. stocks jump as employers unexpectedly cut 23,000 jobs](https://apnews.com/article/9636095906bbb689a1f612bce9a07343)

## 3. Houthi attack claims raise a second shipping-chokepoint risk

**Why it matters:** Escalation around Yemen could pressure the Bab el-Mandeb route while Hormuz is already disrupted. Simultaneous stress at both waterways would leave fewer economical routes for Gulf energy exports and could amplify freight, insurance, and fuel costs. **Action:** Treat casualty and responsibility claims cautiously, but monitor shipping advisories and insurer responses.

**Source:** [AP — How Iran-backed Houthi attack claims are stoking fears of a wider conflict](https://apnews.com/article/6d79ab62dc697281ba5be0352e95b2c5)
